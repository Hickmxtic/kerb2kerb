# Van sign-writing — what goes on the Dispatch

The van is seen most where customers are made: halls car parks in
move-in week and the cruise terminal forecourts 14–15 times a week. Sign-
writing is a one-off cost that advertises every day for free. The copy
below is what to give the sign-writer; the brand colours are the site's.

## Hierarchy (biggest to smallest)

1. **KERB2KERB** — wordmark, both sides + rear doors. Orange `#E05B2B` on
   white/silver, or white on a dark panel. Big enough to read across a car
   park.
2. **Luggage & goods, kerb to kerb** — the positioning line, directly under
   the name. It quietly says "not a taxi" without saying it.
3. **07926 438553** — the WhatsApp number, large, on both sides and the
   rear. People photograph van numbers; make it easy.
4. **kerb2kerb.co.uk** — smaller, both sides.
5. **Southampton · cruise terminals · airports · moves** — one line, rear
   doors only. Names the jobs a person might need right then.

## Rules

- No word that implies passengers ("transfers", "taxi", "lifts"). "Luggage
  & goods" does the work.
- Rear doors carry the number and the web address — that's what the car
  behind you reads at lights.
- WhatsApp logo next to the number is worth it: it tells people how to
  contact you without a call.
- Ask the sign-writer for vinyl, not a wrap — cheaper, and it comes off
  clean if the van is ever sold.
- Get a photo of the finished van the same day: it becomes the site's hero
  image and the first ad creative.

## Optional: mock it up first

A livery mock-up (logo on a Dispatch outline, both sides + rear) is a
15-minute job on the connected Higgsfield brand-asset workflow when James
wants to see it before paying a sign-writer. Not needed to brief one.
